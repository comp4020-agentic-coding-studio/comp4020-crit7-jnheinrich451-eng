# Crit 7 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was turning my own enrolment problem into a workflow with
explicit evidence and outcomes. I began with a permission request that appeared
to be routed under the wrong course code. As I collected the degree,
specialisation and course pages, I found that the larger difficulty was their
relationships: overlapping courses, prerequisites, different years and limited
semester offerings. A course description alone was not enough to decide what a
student could take.

Separating source material, interpreted rules and fictional student records made
the project more useful. Uncertainty could remain visible rather than becoming
an unexplained rejection. The Llama adviser then had a bounded job: match
interests to course evidence while the rule engine checked the suggestions.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to take responsibility for whether a person can understand and complete
the workflow. I kept trying the interface and supplying corrections instead of
treating a functional version as finished. Even after the adviser passed its
checks, I could not find its suggested courses. That prompted a change in where
the answer appeared, not another model prompt.

The direction I want to develop is combining domain understanding with careful
verification: use agents to build and investigate, retain the evidence behind
decisions, and make the next action clear to the user. For this prototype,
real email delivery and the deployed experience still need that same scrutiny.
