# Crit 7 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was treating my own enrolment problem as a workflow where every
outcome comes with its evidence. I started from a permission request that seemed
to be routed under the wrong course code. Once I'd collected the degree,
specialisation and course pages, I realised the harder problem was how they
relate: overlapping courses, prerequisites, changes between years and limited
semester offerings. A course description alone isn't enough to decide what a
student can take.

Keeping the source material, the rules interpreted from it and the fictional
student records apart made the project far more useful. Uncertainty could stay
visible instead of becoming a rejection nobody could explain. It also gave the
Llama adviser a clear, limited job: match interests to course evidence and let
the rule engine check its suggestions.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to take responsibility for whether someone can actually understand the
workflow and get through it. I kept using the interface myself and sending
corrections instead of calling it done once it worked. Even after the adviser
passed its checks, I couldn't find the courses it suggested. The fix was to
change where the answer appeared, not to write another model prompt.

That's the direction I want to grow in: pairing domain understanding with
careful checking. Use agents to build and investigate, keep the evidence behind
decisions, and make the next step obvious to the user. For this prototype, real
email delivery and the deployed site still need that same scrutiny.
